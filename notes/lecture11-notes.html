MMWU for Balanced Separator

A heat-kernel center, the primal-dual loop, O(log n) rounds, and computing the heat kernel

Notes for Lecture 11 (11/3).

These notes accompany the Lecture 11 deck. They give full proofs of the certificates and of the round count, a proof sketch of the rounding lemma, and the material on computing matrix exponentials that the slides only summarize. The algorithm follows the approach of Orecchia–Sachdeva–Vishnoi (STOC 2012); the constants (\(16/b\), \(2\gamma\), \(\tau_0=8\ln n/\gamma\), \(\eta=1/(4\gamma)\), \(T=\lceil32\ln n\rceil\)) are chosen to make each step a one-line calculation and differ from the paper’s.

Notation. \(G=(V,E,w)\) with degrees \(d\), \(\mathrm{vol}(V)=\sum_id_i\), \(\pi=d/\mathrm{vol}(V)\), \(\Pi=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)\), and normalized Laplacian \(\mathcal L=D^{-1/2}LD^{-1/2}\). Its bottom eigenvector is \(\sqrt\pi\); \(W=\sqrt\pi^\perp\) and \(P\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(W\). For \(S\subseteq V\), \(\Phi(S)=w(S,\bar S)/\min(\mathrm{vol}S,\mathrm{vol}\bar S)\) and \(\bar\phi(S)=w(S,\bar S)\,\mathrm{vol}(V)/(\mathrm{vol}S\,\mathrm{vol}\bar S)\in[\Phi(S),2\Phi(S)]\). \(S\) is \(b\)-balanced if \(b\le\pi(S)\le1-b\). Throughout, \(b\in(0,\frac12]\) and \(\gamma\in(0,1]\).

1. The relaxation as a problem over density matrices

Lecture 10 wrote \(\mathrm{SDP}(b)\) in terms of vectors \(v_i\) with Gram matrix \(X\). With \(Y=\Pi^{1/2}X\Pi^{1/2}\):

  • \(\operatorname{tr}Y=\sum_i\pi_i\|v_i\|^2\), so the normalization is \(\operatorname{tr}Y=1\);
  • \(Y\sqrt\pi=\Pi^{1/2}X\pi\), so centering \(\sum_i\pi_iv_i=0\) is \(Y\sqrt\pi=0\), i.e. \(Y\) lives on \(W\);
  • \(\langle L,X\rangle/\mathrm{vol}(V)=\langle\Pi^{-1/2}L\Pi^{-1/2},Y\rangle/\mathrm{vol}(V)=\langle\mathcal L,Y\rangle\);
  • the spread constraint \(\|v_i\|^2\le1/b\) is \(Y_{ii}\le\pi_i/b\).

So \(\mathrm{SDP}(b)=\min\{\langle\mathcal L,Y\rangle: Y\in\Delta(W),\ Y_{ii}\le\pi_i/b\}\), where \(\Delta(W)\) is the set of density matrices supported on \(W\). Lecture 10 showed \(\mathrm{SDP}(b)\le\min\{\bar\phi(S):S\ b\text{-balanced}\}\) using the cut embedding \(y=(\mathbf 1_S-p)/\sqrt{p(1-p)}\), \(p=\pi(S)\in[b,\frac12]\), for which \(\sum_i\pi_iy_i=0\), \(\sum_i\pi_iy_i^2=1\), \(y_i^2\le\max\{\frac{1-p}p,\frac p{1-p}\}\le\frac1b\), and \(y^\top Ly/\mathrm{vol}(V)=\bar\phi(S)\).

The dual. Attach \(\alpha\) to \(\operatorname{tr}Y=1\) and \(\beta_i\ge0\) to \(Y_{ii}\le\pi_i/b\). The dual is \[\max\Big\{\alpha-\frac1b\sum_i\beta_i\pi_i\ :\ \mathcal L+\operatorname{diag}(\beta)\succeq\alpha I\text{ on }W,\ \beta\ge0\Big\}.\] Weak duality: for feasible \(Y\) and dual \((\alpha,\beta)\), \(\langle\mathcal L,Y\rangle\ge\alpha\operatorname{tr}Y-\langle\operatorname{diag}\beta,Y\rangle\ge\alpha-\sum_i\beta_i\pi_i/b\).

2. Penalties, the center, and the cut as a comparator

For \(H\subseteq V\) let \(\Delta_H=P\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf 1_H)P\). For \(x=\Pi^{1/2}y\in W\), \[x^\top\Delta_Hx=\sum_{i\in H}x_i^2=\sum_{i\in H}\pi_iy_i^2 .\] Since \(\sum_i\pi_iy_i=0\), this is the energy of edges of weight \(\pi_i\) joining each \(i\in H\) to an extra vertex held at the mean value \(0\) (“ground”). In particular \(D^{1/2}\big(c\,\mathcal L+\sum_tc_t\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf 1_{H_t})\big)D^{1/2}=cL+\operatorname{diag}(\cdot)\) is a grounded Laplacian (SDD with nonpositive off-diagonal entries).

The center. Let \(Z=\operatorname{tr}_We^{-\tau_0\mathcal L}=\sum_{k\ge2}e^{-\tau_0\lambda_k}\le n-1\) and \(C=e^{-\tau_0\mathcal L}/Z\) on \(W\), a full-rank density matrix on \(W\) with \(\log C=-\tau_0\mathcal L-(\ln Z)I\) there. By Lecture 10 (notes §5, “Centers”), FTRL over density matrices on \(W\) with regularizer \(D(\cdot\Vert C)\) and losses \(M_t\) plays \(Y_t\propto\exp(\log C-\eta S_{t-1})\propto e^{-\tau_0\mathcal L-\eta S_{t-1}}\), and for every density matrix \(U\) on \(W\), if \(-\eta M_t\preceq I\), \[\sum_t\langle M_t,Y_t\rangle\le\Big\langle\sum_tM_t,U\Big\rangle+\eta\sum_t\langle M_t^2,Y_t\rangle+\frac{D(U\Vert C)}\eta .\tag{2.1}\]

Lemma 2.1 (the cut as a comparator). Let \(S\) be \(b\)-balanced; replacing \(S\) by \(\bar S\) if needed, \(p=\pi(S)\in[b,\frac12]\). Let \(x=\Pi^{1/2}y\) with \(y\) the cut embedding and \(U_S=xx^\top\). Then \(U_S\) is a density matrix on \(W\) and

  1. \(\langle\mathcal L,U_S\rangle=\bar\phi(S)\); (ii) \(\langle\Delta_H,U_S\rangle\le\pi(H)/b\) for every \(H\); (iii) \(D(U_S\Vert C)=\tau_0\bar\phi(S)+\ln Z\le\tau_0\bar\phi(S)+\ln n\).

Proof. \(x\in W\) and \(\|x\|^2=\sum_i\pi_iy_i^2=1\). (i) is §1. (ii) \(x^\top\Delta_Hx=\sum_{i\in H}\pi_iy_i^2\le\pi(H)\max_iy_i^2\le\pi(H)/b\). (iii) \(U_S\) has eigenvalues \(1,0,\dots,0\), so \(\operatorname{tr}U_S\log U_S=0\) and \(D(U_S\Vert C)=-\langle\log C,U_S\rangle=\tau_0x^\top\mathcal Lx+\ln Z\). \(\square\)

3. The loop and its certificates

BalSep\((G,b,\gamma)\). Set \(\tau_0=8\ln n/\gamma\), \(\eta=1/(4\gamma)\), \(T=\lceil32\ln n\rceil\), \(S_0=0\). For \(t=1,\dots,T\): let \(Y_t=e^{-\tau_0\mathcal L-\eta S_{t-1}}/\operatorname{tr}(\cdot)\), computed on \(W\); let \(\|v_i\|^2=(Y_t)_{ii}/\pi_i\) and \(H_t=\{i:\|v_i\|^2\ge16/b\}\).

    1. If \(\langle\mathcal L,Y_t\rangle\ge\gamma\), stop and certify.
    1. Else, if \(\sum_{i\in H_t}\pi_i\|v_i\|^2\ge\frac12\), set \(M_t=2\gamma\Delta_{H_t}\) and \(S_t=S_{t-1}+M_t\).
    1. Else round \(Y_t\) (§4) and stop.

If all \(T\) rounds are case (c), certify.

Lemma 3.1. \(\pi(H_t)\le b/16\); in case (c), \(\langle M_t,Y_t\rangle\ge\gamma\); and \(0\preceq M_t\preceq2\gamma I\).

Proof. \(\frac{16}b\pi(H_t)\le\sum_{i\in H_t}\pi_i\|v_i\|^2\le\operatorname{tr}Y_t=1\). Since \(PY_tP=Y_t\), \(\langle\Delta_{H_t},Y_t\rangle=\sum_{i\in H_t}(Y_t)_{ii}=\sum_{i\in H_t}\pi_i\|v_i\|^2\ge\frac12\). Finally \(0\preceq P\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf 1_H)P\preceq P\preceq I\). \(\square\)

In particular, by Lemma 2.1(ii), \(\langle M_s,U_S\rangle\le2\gamma\cdot\frac1{16}=\frac\gamma8\) for every penalty and every \(b\)-balanced \(S\).

Theorem 3.2 (case (a) certifies). If \(\langle\mathcal L,Y_t\rangle\ge\gamma\) for some \(t\le T\), then every \(b\)-balanced \(S\) has \(\bar\phi(S)\ge\frac{3\gamma}4\).

Proof. By Gibbs (Lecture 10, Lemma 2.1, with \(A=\log C-\eta S_{t-1}\)), \(Y_t\) minimizes \(F(Y)=\eta\langle S_{t-1},Y\rangle+D(Y\Vert C)\) over density matrices on \(W\). Hence \(F(Y_t)\le F(U_S)\). On the left, \(\eta\langle S_{t-1},Y_t\rangle\ge0\) and \(D(Y_t\Vert C)=\operatorname{tr}Y_t\log Y_t+\tau_0\langle\mathcal L,Y_t\rangle+\ln Z\ge-\ln n+\tau_0\langle\mathcal L,Y_t\rangle+\ln Z\). On the right, \(\eta\langle S_{t-1},U_S\rangle\le\eta(t-1)\frac\gamma8=\frac{t-1}{32}\le\ln n\) (since \(t-1\le T-1\le32\ln n\)), and \(D(U_S\Vert C)=\tau_0\bar\phi(S)+\ln Z\). Cancelling \(\ln Z\), \[\tau_0\langle\mathcal L,Y_t\rangle\le\tau_0\bar\phi(S)+2\ln n,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\langle\mathcal L,Y_t\rangle\le\bar\phi(S)+\frac\gamma4 .\ \square\]

Theorem 3.3 (running out certifies). If all \(T\) rounds are case (c), then every \(b\)-balanced \(S\) has \(\bar\phi(S)\ge\frac\gamma4\).

Proof. Apply (2.1) with \(U=U_S\); the condition \(-\eta M_t\preceq I\) holds since \(M_t\succeq0\). By Lemma 3.1, \(M_t^2\preceq2\gamma M_t\), so \(\eta\sum_t\langle M_t^2,Y_t\rangle\le\frac12\sum_t\langle M_t,Y_t\rangle\), and \[\frac12\sum_t\langle M_t,Y_t\rangle\le\sum_t\langle M_t,U_S\rangle+4\gamma\,D(U_S\Vert C).\] The left side is at least \(\frac{T\gamma}2\); the right side is at most \(\frac{T\gamma}8+4\gamma(\tau_0\bar\phi(S)+\ln n)=\frac{T\gamma}8+32\ln n\,\bar\phi(S)+4\gamma\ln n\). Since \(T\ge32\ln n\), \(\frac{3T\gamma}8\ge12\gamma\ln n\), hence \(32\ln n\,\bar\phi(S)\ge8\gamma\ln n\). \(\square\)

Since \(\Phi\ge\bar\phi/2\), any certificate gives \(\Phi(S)\ge\gamma/8\) for every \(b\)-balanced \(S\).

As a dual solution. Apply (2.1) instead with \(U=uu^\top\), \(u\) a bottom eigenvector of \(A_T=\tau_0\mathcal L+\eta S_T\) on \(W\): then \(D(U\Vert C)=\tau_0u^\top\mathcal Lu+\ln Z\) and \(\frac{\eta T\gamma}2\le\eta u^\top S_Tu+\tau_0u^\top\mathcal Lu+\ln Z=\lambda_{\min}(A_T)+\ln Z\), so \(\lambda_{\min}(A_T)\ge\frac T8-\ln n\ge3\ln n\). Dividing by \(\tau_0\): \(\mathcal L+\operatorname{diag}(\beta)\succeq\frac{3\gamma}8I\) on \(W\) with \(\beta=\frac{2\gamma\eta}{\tau_0}\sum_t\mathbf 1_{H_t}\) (quadratic forms of \(P\operatorname{diag}(\beta)P\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(\beta)\) agree on \(W\)). Its dual value is at least \(\frac{3\gamma}8-\frac{2\gamma\eta}{\tau_0}\cdot\frac{T}{16}=\frac{3\gamma}8-\frac{\gamma T}{256\ln n}\ge\frac\gamma4-\frac\gamma{256\ln n}\) (using \(T\le32\ln n+1\)), matching Theorem 3.3 up to the rounding of \(T\).

Why the center. Without it (\(C=I/n\), plays \(e^{-\eta S_{t-1}}\)), a high-energy round cannot be turned into a certificate, because \(D(U_S\Vert I/n)=\ln n\) carries no information about \(\bar\phi(S)\). It must instead be played as the loss \(\mathcal L\), which has width \(2\) and gain only \(\gamma\). The local-norm bound then needs \(\eta\le\frac14\) and \(T=\Theta(\ln n/\gamma)\) rounds, and the plays reach heat-kernel time \(\tau=\eta T_a=\Theta(\ln n/\gamma)\) only after those rounds. The center starts the plays at that time for free and charges \(D(U_S\Vert C)/\eta=4\gamma(\tau_0\bar\phi(S)+\ln Z)\), which is \(O(\gamma\ln n)\), i.e. the gain of \(O(\ln n)\) rounds, exactly when \(\bar\phi(S)=O(\gamma)\) (equivalently, \(D(U_S\Vert C)=O(\ln n)\) iff \(\bar\phi(S)=O(\gamma)\)). The remaining losses (penalties) have gain/width ratio \(\frac12\), so \(O(\ln n)\) rounds suffice, independently of \(\gamma\).

What the plays are. \(Y_t\propto\exp\big(-\tau_0(\mathcal L+\frac{2\gamma\eta}{\tau_0}\sum_{s<t}\Delta_{H_s})\big)\): the (\(\sqrt\pi\)-projected) heat kernel at time \(\tau_0\) of \(G\) with extra edges to ground of weight \(\frac{2\gamma\eta}{\tau_0}|\{s<t:i\in H_s\}|\,\pi_i=\frac{|\{s<t:i\in H_s\}|}{16\ln n}\,\gamma\,\pi_i\) at vertex \(i\) (in the scaling of the quadratic form). The walk is killed at a rate that grows each time a vertex is heavy.

4. Rounding a spread play (sketch)

Lemma 4.1 (rounding). Suppose \(\langle\mathcal L,Y\rangle<\gamma\) and the light vertices \(\{i:\|v_i\|^2<16/b\}\) carry at least half of \(\sum_i\pi_i\|v_i\|^2=1\). Let \(g\) be a standard Gaussian vector and \(y_i=\langle v_i,g\rangle\). With constant probability, some sweep cut of \(y\) is \(\Omega(b)\)-balanced with \(\Phi=O(\sqrt{\gamma})\), where the constants depend only on \(b\).

Sketch. This is the step where we have not optimized constants, and the argument below should be checked against OSV §3 (and Orecchia–Vishnoi 2011) before it is relied on.

  1. Projection. \(\mathbb E\,y_i^2=\|v_i\|^2\) and \(\mathbb E(y_i-y_j)^2=\|v_i-v_j\|^2\). So \(\mathbb E\sum_{ij}w_{ij}(y_i-y_j)^2=\mathrm{vol}(V)\langle\mathcal L,Y\rangle\) and \(\mathbb E\sum_i\pi_iy_i^2=1\); also \(\sum_i\pi_iy_i=0\) exactly. By Markov on the energy and a second-moment (Paley–Zygmund) bound on the light part of the variance, with constant probability the energy is \(O(\gamma)\mathrm{vol}(V)\) and the light vertices carry \(\Omega(1)\) of \(\sum\pi_iy_i^2\), while \(\sum_{\text{light}}\pi_iy_i^2\mathbf 1[y_i^2>C/b]\) is a small fraction of it for a large constant \(C\) (Gaussian tails, since \(\mathbb E y_i^2<16/b\) there).
  2. Truncation. Let \(z_i=\max(-a,\min(y_i,a))\) with \(a=\sqrt{C/b}\). Truncation is \(1\)-Lipschitz, so the energy of \(z\) is at most that of \(y\), and \(\sum_i\pi_iz_i^2=\Omega(1)\) by step 1. Shifting \(z\) by a median-type constant \(\theta\) (chosen so that \(\pi\{z>\theta\}\le\frac12\) and \(\pi\{z<\theta\}\le\frac12\)) keeps a constant fraction of the variance.
  3. Volume. Since \(|z_i-\theta|\le2a\), the set where \((z_i-\theta)^2\ge c\) has volume at least \((\Omega(1)-c)/(4a^2)=\Omega(b)\) for a small constant \(c\).
  4. Sweep. Apply the co-area/Cauchy–Schwarz argument of Cheeger’s inequality (Lecture 6) to the positive and negative parts of \(z-\theta\), but only over thresholds \(r\) with \(r^2\ge c\). The level sets over that range have volume between \(\Omega(b)\) (step 3) and \(\frac12\) (choice of \(\theta\)), and the argument produces one with \(\Phi=O(\sqrt{\text{energy}/\text{variance}})=O(\sqrt\gamma)\), with the constant depending on \(b\) through \(a\) and \(c\).

On the whisker example (script lectures/lecture11/scripts/loop_figure.py, \(b=\frac14\)) the first play is already spread and 20 projections give, for \(\gamma=0.02\), a cut with \(\Phi=0.0235\) and balance \(0.49\), and for \(\gamma=0.05\) a two-whisker cut with \(\Phi=0.024\) and balance \(0.095\).

5. Computing the plays

What is needed. Each round uses \(\|v_i\|^2\) for all \(i\), the energy \(\langle\mathcal L,Y_t\rangle=\frac1{\mathrm{vol}(V)}\sum_{ij}w_{ij}\|v_i-v_j\|^2\), and, in case (b), the projections \(y_i=\langle v_i,g\rangle\). With \(A=\tau_0\mathcal L+\eta S_{t-1}\) (on \(W\)) and \(Y_t^{1/2}=e^{-A/2}/\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}e^{-A}}\), a valid choice of vectors is \(v_i=\pi_i^{-1/2}\,e_i^\top Y_t^{1/2}\).

Johnson–Lindenstrauss. Let \(U\) be \(n\times k\) with i.i.d. \(N(0,1/k)\) entries, \(k=O(\log n/\varepsilon^2)\). With high probability the vectors \(\tilde v_i=\pi_i^{-1/2}e_i^\top Y_t^{1/2}U\) satisfy \(\|\tilde v_i\|^2=(1\pm\varepsilon)\|v_i\|^2\) and \(\|\tilde v_i-\tilde v_j\|^2=(1\pm\varepsilon)\|v_i-v_j\|^2\) for all the \(O(n+m)\) pairs needed. The trace is estimated the same way: \(\operatorname{tr}e^{-A}=\|e^{-A/2}\|_F^2\). Each round therefore needs \(k=O(\log n)\) products \(e^{-A/2}u\); an approximation error \(\delta\) in these products perturbs the gains by \(O(\delta)\), which is absorbed by running the oracle with \(\gamma/2\) in place of \(\gamma\).

The matrix. \(A=\tau_0\mathcal L+\eta S_{t-1}\), so \(\|A\|\le2\tau_0+2\gamma\eta T=\frac{16\ln n}\gamma+\frac T2=O(\log n/\gamma)\). On \(W\), \(A=PA'P\) with \(A'=\tau_0\mathcal L+\operatorname{diag}(\beta')\), \(\beta'\ge0\), and \(D^{1/2}A'D^{1/2}=\tau_0L+D\operatorname{diag}(\beta')\) is a grounded Laplacian. Note \(Pe^{-A'}P\ne e^{-PA'P}\) in general (the penalties break the eigenvector \(\sqrt\pi\)), so the projection must be handled inside each solve. For \(u\in W\), the solution \(z\in W\) of \((I+PA'P/k)z=u\) is \(z=B^{-1}u+cB^{-1}\sqrt\pi\) with \(B=I+A'/k\) and \(c=-\langle\sqrt\pi,B^{-1}u\rangle/\langle\sqrt\pi,B^{-1}\sqrt\pi\rangle\): then \(z\perp\sqrt\pi\) and \(PBz=u\). So each solve costs two SDD solves with \(B\), \(\tilde O(m)\) time (Lecture 9). (OSV instead use star penalties, whose Laplacians annihilate \(\sqrt\pi\), which avoids the issue.)

Polynomial approximation. For \(x\in[0,\beta]\) there is a polynomial of degree \(O\big(\sqrt{\max(\beta,\log\frac1\delta)\log\frac1\delta}\big)\) with \(|e^{-x}-p(x)|\le\delta\) (Chebyshev; see Sachdeva–Vishnoi). Lanczos finds an approximation at least as good without knowing \(p\). With \(\beta=\|A\|=O(\log n/\gamma)\) this is \(\tilde O(1/\sqrt\gamma)\) matrix-vector products, i.e. \(\tilde O(m/\sqrt\gamma)\) per product \(e^{-A/2}u\).

Rational approximation. Saff–Schönhage–Varga: there are polynomials \(p_k\) of degree \(k\) with \[\sup_{x\ge0}\big|e^{-x}-p_k\big((1+x/k)^{-1}\big)\big|\le2^{-\Omega(k)} .\] So \(k=O(\log\frac1\delta)\) suffices uniformly on \([0,\infty)\), independently of \(\|A\|\). Evaluating \(p_k((I+A/k)^{-1})u\) takes \(k\) solves with \(I+A/k\) on \(W\), each two SDD solves by the bordered system above; OSV run Lanczos on \((I+A/k)^{-1}\) and show that approximate solves suffice. Cost: \(\tilde O(m)\) per product. Precision. The plays are normalized by \(\operatorname{tr}e^{-A}\ge e^{-\lambda_{\min}(A)}\), so we need \(\delta\le n^{-O(1)}e^{-\lambda_{\min}(A)}\). Before any penalty \(\lambda_{\min}(A)=\tau_0\lambda_2\), and if \(\lambda_2\ge\gamma\) then \(\langle\mathcal L,Y_1\rangle\ge\lambda_2\ge\gamma\) and case (a) certifies in round 1; otherwise \(\lambda_{\min}(A)\le\tau_0\gamma+\frac T2=O(\ln n)\) throughout (penalties add at most \(2\gamma\eta T=\frac T2\)), so \(k=O(\log n)\) suffices.

Total. \(T=O(\log n)\) rounds, \(O(\log n)\) products per round, \(\tilde O(m)\) per product: \(\tilde O(m)\), independent of \(\gamma\). With the polynomial approximation instead, the large starting time \(\tau_0\) costs a factor \(\sqrt{\|A\|}\): \(\tilde O(m/\sqrt\gamma)\). Without the center (§3, “Why the center”) the round count alone would add a factor \(1/\gamma\).

6. What to check against the paper

  • The paper’s oracle and penalty normalization (stars vs. the diagonal penalties \(\Delta_H\) used here), its heavy-set threshold, and whether it also uses radial sweeps to find unbalanced sparse cuts.
  • The exact statement of the rounding lemma and its constants (§4).
  • How the paper initializes the walk (our center \(e^{-\tau_0\mathcal L}\) with \(\tau_0=\Theta(\log n/\gamma)\)) and its iteration count (ours is \(\lceil32\ln n\rceil\)).

7. Exercises

  1. Show \(\bar\phi(S)\in[\Phi(S),2\Phi(S)]\), and that the cut embedding satisfies \(y_i^2\le1/b\) when \(\pi(S)\in[b,\frac12]\).
  2. Verify the dual of \(\mathrm{SDP}(b)\) in §1 and the dual solution extracted from the certificate.
  3. Redo Theorems 3.2 and 3.3 with heavy threshold \(c/b\), penalty weight \(\kappa\gamma\), starting time \(\tau_0=\alpha\ln n/\gamma\) and step size \(\eta=\theta/\gamma\). For which parameters do both go through, and how do \(T\) and the certificate constant depend on them?
  4. Run the loop without a center (\(C=I/n\)), playing high-energy rounds as losses \(\mathcal L\). Prove a round bound of \(O(\log n/\gamma)\) with the local-norm bound, and show that the uncentered regret bound cannot give \(O(\log n)\).
  5. Construct a family of graphs on which case (c) occurs in BalSep. (Half of the variance of a low-energy heat kernel must sit on vertices with \(\|v_i\|^2\ge16/b\).)
  6. Prove the Johnson–Lindenstrauss claim in §5, including the estimate of \(\operatorname{tr}e^{-A}\). ## References
