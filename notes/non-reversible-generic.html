Non-Reversible Walks and the Dissipative/Poisson Split

Directed graphs, probability currents, and GENERIC

Draft. For the lecture on directed graphs and non-reversible Markov chains.

Lecture 2 (§2.4 of the lecture notes) flagged this and moved on: “Without reversibility, \(h_t\) evolves under \(H_t^*\), the semigroup of the time-reversed chain… we’ll redo this for general Markov semigroups.” This note is that redo. The punchline: every random walk on a directed graph splits canonically into a reversible part (all of Lecture 2’s machinery applies unchanged) and a pure-circulation part (an isometry — no dissipation at all), and this is exactly the Poisson/dissipative split of GENERIC, the formalism used in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics to combine reversible and irreversible dynamics.

Notation follows the lecture notes: \(P\) is the one-step transition matrix of an irreducible chain on \(G=(V,E,w)\), now directed, with unique stationary distribution \(\pi\) (Perron–Frobenius). We do not assume detailed balance.

1. The adjoint is the reversed chain

Definition. The time-reversed chain is \(\hat P := \Pi^{-1}P^\top\Pi\), i.e. \(\pi_i\hat P_{ij} = \pi_jP_{ji}\). (This is the one-step version of \(H_t^*=\Pi^{-1}H_t^\top\Pi\) from Lecture 2 §2.4.) \(\hat P\) is itself a stochastic matrix with the same stationary distribution \(\pi\): \(\pi^\top\hat P = \pi^\top\) iff \(\sum_i\pi_i\hat P_{ij}=\sum_i\pi_jP_{ji}=\pi_j\sum_iP_{ji}=\pi_j\). ✓.

Proposition. \(\hat P\) is the \(L^2(\pi)\)-adjoint of \(P\): \(\langle f,Pg\rangle_\pi=\langle\hat Pf,g\rangle_\pi\).

Proof. \(\langle f,Pg\rangle_\pi = \sum_{i,j}\pi_iP_{ij}f_ig_j\) and \(\langle\hat Pf,g\rangle_\pi=\sum_{i,j}\pi_j\hat P_{ji}f_ig_j\); the definition of \(\hat P\) (with \(i,j\) swapped) gives \(\pi_j\hat P_{ji}=\pi_iP_{ij}\). \(\square\)

\(G\) is reversible iff \(P=\hat P\), iff \(L_{rw}=L_{rw}^*\) (self-adjoint) — the case the whole of Lecture 2 handles. Write \(\hat L_{rw}:=I-\hat P = L_{rw}^*\) for the Laplacian of the reversed chain in general.

2. The symmetric/antisymmetric split

Definition. \(\bar P := \tfrac12(P+\hat P)\) (the additive reversibilization), and \[ L_{rw}^{\rm sym} := \tfrac12(L_{rw}+L_{rw}^*) = I-\bar P, \qquad L_{rw}^{\rm anti} := \tfrac12(L_{rw}-L_{rw}^*) = \tfrac12(\hat P-P), \] so \(L_{rw} = L_{rw}^{\rm sym}+L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\), with \(L_{rw}^{\rm sym}\) self-adjoint and \(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\) skew-adjoint in \(L^2(\pi)\) (standard decomposition of any operator).

Proposition. \(\bar P\) is a reversible chain with stationary distribution \(\pi\).

Proof. \(\bar P\) is stochastic (average of two stochastic matrices) and \(\pi_i\bar P_{ij} = \tfrac12(\pi_iP_{ij}+\pi_i\hat P_{ij}) = \tfrac12(\pi_iP_{ij}+\pi_jP_{ji})\), symmetric under \(i\leftrightarrow j\). \(\square\)

So \(L_{rw}^{\rm sym}\) is the random-walk Laplacian of a genuine reversible graph — every fact from Lecture 2 (self-adjointness, PSD, Dirichlet form \(\mathcal E^{\rm sym}(f,g):=\langle f,L_{rw}^{\rm sym}g\rangle_\pi\), Poincaré, \(\chi^2\)-decay, Proof 2, the gradient-flow picture) applies to \(\bar P\) unchanged. All the “dissipation” lives here.

\(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\), being skew-adjoint, generates a one-parameter group of isometries of \(L^2(\pi)\): \(\|e^{-tL_{rw}^{\rm anti}}f\|_\pi = \|f\|_\pi\) for all \(t\) (standard fact: \(\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{tA}f\|^2 = 2\langle Af,f\rangle=0\) when \(A^*=-A\), since a skew form vanishes on the diagonal). No distance to \(\pi\) shrinks under this flow alone — it only moves mass around, in loops.

3. Probability currents

Definition. The (stationary) current across the directed pair \((i,j)\) is \(J_{ij} := \pi_iP_{ij}-\pi_jP_{ji}\).

Proposition. \((L_{rw}^{\rm anti})_{ij} = -J_{ij}/(2\pi_i)\) for \(i\ne j\). In particular \(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\equiv0\) iff \(J\equiv0\) iff detailed balance holds.

Proof. \(\hat P_{ij}=\pi_jP_{ji}/\pi_i\) from §1, so \((L_{rw}^{\rm anti})_{ij}=\tfrac12(\hat P_{ij}-P_{ij}) = \tfrac{1}{2\pi_i}(\pi_jP_{ji}-\pi_iP_{ij}) = -J_{ij}/(2\pi_i)\). \(\square\)

A nonzero stationary current is exactly the signature of a non-equilibrium steady state (NESS) in the statistical-mechanics sense: the system has settled into a stationary distribution, but mass is still perpetually circulating around directed cycles rather than sitting still. (Kirchhoff-type fact, not proved here: \(J\) is divergence-free, \(\sum_jJ_{ij}=0\) for every \(i\) — stationary currents have no sources or sinks, so they decompose into a sum of circulations around cycles. Good exercise.)

4. This is the GENERIC / metriplectic split

GENERIC (Grmela–Öttinger; “metriplectic,” Morrison) writes dynamics as \(\dot x = \{x,E\}+(x,S)\): a Poisson bracket \(\{\cdot,\cdot\}\) (antisymmetric, generates reversible/energy-conserving motion) plus a dissipative bracket \((\cdot,\cdot)\) (symmetric, semi-definite, generates irreversible relaxation), with degeneracy conditions \(\{E,\cdot\}=\{\cdot,S\}=0\)-type statements linking the two.

Here, with \(h_t=H_th_0\) densities and \(\partial_th_t=-L_{rw}h_t = -L_{rw}^{\rm sym}h_t - L_{rw}^{\rm anti}h_t\):

  • \(-L_{rw}^{\rm sym}h_t\) is the familiar gradient flow \(-\nabla_\pi F(h_t)\) of \(F(h)=\tfrac12\mathcal E^{\rm sym}(h,h)\) — the dissipative part.
  • \(-L_{rw}^{\rm anti}h_t\) is generated by a skew-adjoint operator — the Poisson/circulatory part.
  • Both parts conserve total mass individually (\(L_{rw}^{\rm sym}\mathbf1=L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\mathbf1=0\) — check the second: \(\hat P\mathbf1=P\mathbf1=\mathbf1\)), the discrete analogue of GENERIC’s energy-conservation degeneracy condition, with \(E(h):=\mathbb E_\pi h\) as the conserved quantity.

Caveat — don’t oversell this. The full GENERIC axiom would further require the Poisson part to leave the dissipative potential \(F\) itself exactly invariant, \(\langle\nabla_\pi F, L_{rw}^{\rm anti}h\rangle_\pi \equiv 0\). This holds iff \(L_{rw}^{\rm sym}\) and \(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\) commute — not automatic for a general directed graph. What’s unconditional is the weaker mass-conservation above, plus (next section) comparison results at the level of the spectral gap rather than instantaneous conservation.

5. Worked example: the directed cycle

Let \(P\) be the deterministic directed \(n\)-cycle, \(P_{i,i+1}=1\). Then:

  • \(\pi\) = uniform (the only stationary distribution of a permutation matrix on one cycle).
  • \(\hat P_{i,i-1}=1\): the reversed chain is the deterministic backward cycle.
  • \(\bar P = \tfrac12(P+\hat P)\): step to \(i+1\) or \(i-1\) each w.p. \(\tfrac12\) — exactly simple random walk on the undirected cycle \(C_n\), whose spectral gap was computed in Lecture 2 §6.2: \(\lambda(\bar L_{rw}) = 1-\cos(2\pi/n)\approx 2\pi^2/n^2\).
  • \(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\) is pure rotation: in the Fourier/circulant eigenbasis already used for \(C_n\) in §6.2, \(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\) is diagonal with purely imaginary eigenvalues (skew-adjoint operators are diagonalized by the same unitary eigenbasis that diagonalizes any circulant, with eigenvalues on the imaginary axis).

So: the directed cycle’s walk is literally “simple random walk on \(C_n\), plus a clock.” All the dissipation is the \(C_n\) relaxation already computed; the directed-ness just adds a rotation that never affects any \(\pi\)-weighted quadratic functional (since here \(L_{rw}^{\rm sym}\) and \(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\) do commute — both are circulant, hence simultaneously diagonalized by the Fourier basis — so this example sits in the clean/non-generic case of §4’s caveat). Good first example to put on the board; a good second example is a directed cycle with unequal edge weights, where \(\bar P\ne\) plain SRW and the commuting property generally fails.

6. Why this is useful, not just cute

Adding a measure-preserving antisymmetric perturbation to a reversible chain is a known technique for accelerating mixing without changing the stationary distribution: it provably never increases, and generically decreases, relaxation time / asymptotic MCMC variance. Classical in continuous space (Hwang–Hwang–Sheu, accelerating Langevin diffusions by adding a divergence-free drift); discrete/finite-chain and “lifting” versions since (Diaconis–Holmes–Neal; Chen–Lovász–Pak). This is the finite-state cousin of underdamped vs. overdamped Langevin dynamics. (Citation details from memory: check before lecturing.)

Open questions worth posing in class:

  1. Given a target \(\pi\) and reversible \(\bar P\), how large can you make \(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\) (i.e. how much current can you inject) while keeping \(\pi\) fixed, and does the spectral gap of the non-symmetric \(L_{rw}\) (suitably defined, e.g. via the real part of its spectrum) always dominate \(\lambda(L_{rw}^{\rm sym})\)?
  2. Does the commuting / clean case of §5 generalize beyond vertex-transitive graphs?
  3. Connection to Lecture 2 §5.3’s discrete transport metric (Maas, Mielke): does their gradient-flow-of-entropy picture have a GENERIC-style analogue for non-reversible chains?

7. Hypocoercivity: the genuinely degenerate regime

§6 is the easy regime: for a connected directed graph, \(\ker L_{rw}^{\rm sym}\) is always just the constants (1-dimensional), so \(L_{rw}^{\rm sym}\) alone already has a spectral gap, and \(L_{rw}^{\rm anti}\) is a bonus. Villani’s hypocoercivity (2009 AMS Memoir) is the opposite, hard regime: the symmetric part is massively degenerate — a large kernel beyond the constants — and converges only because the antisymmetric part continually feeds information into the directions where dissipation can act. “Hypo” = below/under coercive, same prefix and spirit as Hörmander’s hypoellipticity.

The model problem (kinetic Fokker–Planck). On \((x,v)\in\mathbb R^{2d}\), \[ \partial_tf = \underbrace{\Delta_vf+\nabla_v\!\cdot(vf)}_{L^{\rm sym}:\text{ velocity diffusion}}\ \underbrace{-\,v\cdot\nabla_xf+\nabla_xU\cdot\nabla_vf}_{L^{\rm anti}:\text{ transport/Liouville}}. \] \(\ker L^{\rm sym}\) is every function of \(x\) alone — infinite-dimensional. A function depending only on position is invisible to the dissipation and would never move under \(L^{\rm sym}\) alone. Convergence of the full equation relies entirely on transport (\(v\cdot\nabla_x\)) continually mixing \(x\)-dependence into \(v\)-dependence.

No plain graph random walk can exhibit this — connectedness forces \(\ker L_{rw}^{\rm sym}=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf1)\), always 1-dimensional. To get a genuine finite-dimensional analogue of the kinetic picture, build a lifted chain: state space \(V\times\{\text{auxiliary factor}\}\) (a discrete “velocity” — e.g. a direction bit, or more generally a finite set of momenta), with the symmetric part dissipating only in the auxiliary factor (so \(\ker L^{\rm sym}\) = all functions of \(V\) alone, genuinely large) and the antisymmetric part a discrete transport coupling the two factors (move in \(V\) according to the current auxiliary state, as in discrete/lifted MCMC samplers — now read as the kinetic story rather than the acceleration story of §6). This is the same construction underlying discrete Hamiltonian/lifted MCMC, just with the opposite motivation foregrounded.

The abstract criterion (Dolbeault–Mouhot–Schmeiser form). Write \(L = A^*A + B\) on a Hilbert space, \(A^*A = L^{\rm sym}\succeq0\) with kernel \(\mathcal N\), \(B=L^{\rm anti}\) skew-adjoint. If the commutator \([A,B]\), restricted to \(\mathcal N\), is coercive — \[ \|[A,B]\psi\|\ge\delta\|\psi\|\qquad\text{for all }\psi\in\mathcal N \] — (plus a mild boundedness condition on \([A,B]\) off \(\mathcal N\)), then the modified quadratic form \[ \mathcal H[f] := \|f\|^2 + a\|Af\|^2 + 2b\langle Af,Bf\rangle + c\|Bf\|^2 \] (for suitable small \(a,b,c>0\)) is equivalent to \(\|f\|^2\) and decays exponentially, \(\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal H[f_t]\le-\kappa\mathcal H[f_t]\), even though \(\|f_t\|^2\) alone has no clean dissipation estimate (since \(\langle f,L^{\rm sym}f\rangle=0\) on all of \(\mathcal N\)). Coercivity of \([A,B]\) on \(\mathcal N\) is exactly a one-step Hörmander bracket-generating condition: \(B\) must move every degenerate direction into a dissipated one.

Why this is a good teaching example. In the PDE setting, checking the hypotheses and constructing \(\mathcal H\) involves real functional-analytic care (unbounded operators, domains, weighted Sobolev spaces). For a finite lifted chain, \(A,B\) are just matrices, \(\mathcal N\) a genuine finite-dimensional subspace, and the coercivity condition on \([A,B]|_{\mathcal N}\) is literally a smallest-singular-value computation — no functional analysis overhead. A worked finite example (smallest lifted chain exhibiting the phenomenon: \(V=\) a cycle or path, auxiliary factor \(=\{+1,-1\}\), symmetric part flips the auxiliary bit at some rate, antisymmetric part moves in \(V\) in the direction of the current bit) would make a genuinely good capstone exercise, possibly a nice bridge from this lecture toward later discrete-curvature material (Maas/Erbar, already flagged in Lecture 2’s speaker notes).

Exercise to design: write down the smallest lifted chain on a graph for which \(L^{\rm sym}\) alone is reducible/disconnected on its nontrivial-kernel directions but \(L=L^{\rm sym}+L^{\rm anti}\) has a provable spectral gap via the bracket condition above; compute \(\delta\) and compare the resulting rate to the “naive” (non-lifted) chain’s gap.
