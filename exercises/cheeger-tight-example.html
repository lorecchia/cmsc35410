Exercise: A Tight Example for the Hard Side of Cheeger

Recommended exercise for Lecture 6

Product of a tree and a path: the Fiedler sweep cut loses a square root.

Cheeger’s inequality, \(\tfrac12\lambda \le \Phi(G) \le \sqrt{2\lambda}\), comes with an algorithm: sort the vertices by the Fiedler vector \(f\) and return the best threshold set \(S_t=\{f>t\}\). This set satisfies \(\Phi(S_t)\le\sqrt{2\lambda}\le 2\sqrt{\Phi(G)}\). In this exercise you will build graphs where the algorithm really does lose the square root: every sweep cut of the Fiedler vector has conductance \(\Omega(\sqrt{\Phi(G)})\) (up to a \(\sqrt{\log n}\) factor, which you can remove numerically), although a cut of conductance \(O(1/n)\) exists.

Conventions. Self-loops are allowed. A loop at \(i\) adds its weight to \(d_i\) (so it counts in \(\mathrm{vol}\)) but is never cut and does not change \(L = D - A\). If \(G\) is \(d\)-regular (loops included), then \(L_{rw} = L/d\), so \(\lambda = \lambda_2(L)/d\). For \(f\in\mathbb R^V\), a sweep set of \(f\) is a threshold set \(S_t=\{i : f_i>t\}\) with \(\emptyset\ne S_t\ne V\).

(a) Why not the cycle? The cycle satisfies \(\Phi(C_n)=\Theta(\sqrt{\lambda})\), so the upper side of Cheeger is tight as an inequality. Show nevertheless that on \(C_n\) (\(n\) even) the sweep cut of any vector in the Fiedler eigenspace is an optimal cut. What goes wrong with the cycle as an example for the algorithm?

(b) Products. For graphs \(G, H\), the Cartesian product \(G\square H\) has vertex set \(V(G)\times V(H)\), with \((x,y)\sim(x',y)\) for \(x\sim x'\) in \(G\), and \((x,y)\sim(x,y')\) for \(y\sim y'\) in \(H\) (weights and loops inherited). Show that \[ L_{G\square H} = L_G\otimes I + I\otimes L_H, \] and that if \(L_G u=\mu u\), \(L_H v=\nu v\), then \(u\otimes v\) is an eigenvector of \(L_{G\square H}\) with eigenvalue \(\mu+\nu\). Why does this give all eigenvalues?

(c) The two factors. Let \(T_h\) be the complete binary tree of depth \(h\) on \(n=2^{h+1}-1\) vertices, with loops added so that every vertex has degree \(3\) (a leaf gets two loops, the root one). Let \(P_k\) be the path on \(k\) vertices \(1,\dots,k\), with one loop at each endpoint so every vertex has degree \(2\). Write \(\lambda_T=\lambda_2(L_{T_h})\) and \(\lambda_P=\lambda_2(L_{P_k})\).

  1. Show \(\lambda_T\le \dfrac{2}{n-1}\) (test function: \(+1\) on one subtree of the root, \(-1\) on the other, \(0\) at the root).
  2. Show \(\lambda_T\ge \dfrac{2}{h\,n}\) (canonical paths: route each pair along the unique tree path).
  3. Show \(\lambda_P = 2-2\cos(\pi/k)\le \pi^2/k^2\), with eigenvector \(g_y=\cos\!\big(\pi (y-\tfrac12)/k\big)\), which is strictly decreasing in \(y\).

(d) The cheap cut. Let \(G=T_h\square P_k\). Show that \(G\) is \(5\)-regular, and that \[ \Phi(G)\ \le\ \frac{2}{5(n-1)}. \] (Cut the edge from the root to its left child in every copy of the tree.)

(e) The Fiedler vector points the wrong way. Suppose \(k>\pi\sqrt{hn/2}\). Show that \(\lambda_P<\lambda_T\), and conclude that \[ \lambda=\lambda_2(L_{rw}(G))=\frac{\lambda_P}{5}, \] that this eigenvalue is simple, and that the Fiedler vector is \(F(x,y)=g_y\), which does not depend on the tree coordinate \(x\).

(f) The sweep loses a square root. Show that every sweep set of \(F\) has the form \(S=V(T_h)\times\{1,\dots,j\}\), with \(1\le j\le k-1\), and that \[ \Phi(S)=\frac{1}{5\min\{j,\,k-j\}}\ \ge\ \frac{2}{5k}. \] Take \(k=\lceil \pi\sqrt{hn/2}\,\rceil+1\) and conclude that, for large \(h\), every sweep cut of the Fiedler vector satisfies \[ \Phi(S)\ \ge\ \frac{c}{\sqrt h}\,\sqrt{\Phi(G)},\qquad c\approx 0.28 . \] So the Cheeger rounding cannot be improved by more than a \(\sqrt{\log n}\) factor, for this algorithm.

(g) Numerical check, and removing the log. Build \(G\) explicitly (e.g. with numpy.kron using (b)), for \(h=3,4,5,6\) and \(k=\lceil 1.1\,\pi\sqrt n\,\rceil\). For each case:

  • compute \(\lambda_2(L_{rw}(G))\) and verify it equals \(\lambda_P/5\);
  • compute the Fiedler vector, sweep over its threshold sets, and report the best \(\Phi(S)\);
  • compare with the cut of (d), and with \(\lambda/2\) and \(\sqrt{2\lambda}\).

Plot \(\Phi(S_{\text{best}})\big/\Phi(\text{cut of (d)})\) against \(n\). Numerically \(n\lambda_T\to1\). How does this explain why you needed \(k\approx\pi\sqrt{hn/2}\) in (e), while \(k\approx\pi\sqrt n\) is enough in practice, and what does it say about \(\Phi(S)/\sqrt{\Phi(G)}\) with that choice?

(h) Discussion. The eigenvalue \(\lambda\) says how much of a bottleneck there is, but not where it is. In \(G\) there are two bottlenecks at different scales: a cheap one along the tree (\(\Phi\approx\lambda\), the tree is “large”) and an expensive one along the path (\(\Phi\approx\sqrt\lambda\), the path is “long”). Their eigenvalues almost coincide, and the eigenvector picks the wrong one. Explain how the same phenomenon appears on the hypercube \(Q_d\), where \(\lambda_2\) has multiplicity \(d\), and a random vector in the eigenspace has sweep cuts of conductance \(\approx 1/\sqrt d\) while \(\Phi(Q_d)=1/d\). (No proof required.)

Hint for (a). The Fiedler eigenspace of \(C_n\) is spanned by \(\cos(2\pi i/n)\) and \(\sin(2\pi i/n)\). A combination is \(r\cos(2\pi i/n+\theta)\). What are its threshold sets? What is \(\Phi\) of an arc with \(\ell\) vertices?

Hint for (b). Both \(D\) and \(A\) of the product are sums of Kronecker terms, and \((A\otimes B)(u\otimes v)=Au\otimes Bv\). Products of eigenbases form a basis of \(\mathbb R^{V(G)\times V(H)}\).

Hint for (c). For the lower bound, recall from Lecture 5 the canonical-path inequality: for \(f\perp\mathbf 1\) and paths \(\gamma_{ij}\), \[ n\|f\|^2=\sum_{i<j}(f_i-f_j)^2\le\Big(\max_e\sum_{ij:\,e\in\gamma_{ij}}|\gamma_{ij}|\Big)\,f^\top Lf, \] by Cauchy–Schwarz along each path. A tree edge with \(s\) vertices on one side lies on \(s(n-s)\le (n^2-1)/4\) paths, each of length at most \(2h\). For the path, check directly that \(L_{P_k}g=(2-2\cos\frac\pi k)\,g\), using the boundary loops at the endpoints. Use \(2-2\cos x\le x^2\).

Hint for (d). Each vertex of \(G\) has degree \(3+2=5\). The set \(S\) = (left subtree of the root) \(\times\{1..k\}\) has \(k(n-1)/2\) vertices and \(k\) cut edges.

Hint for (e). By (b), the eigenvalues of \(L_G\) are \(\mu_i+\nu_j\). The smallest nonzero ones are \(\lambda_T\) (from \((x,y)\mapsto f_T(x)\)), \(\lambda_P\) (from \(g_y\)), and \(2-2\cos(2\pi/k)\) (the next path mode). Compare them.

Hint for (f). Because \(g\) is strictly decreasing, \(\{F>t\}=V(T_h)\times\{y\le j\}\). The cut consists of the \(n\) edges between rows \(j\) and \(j+1\), and \(\mathrm{vol}(S)=5jn\), \(\mathrm{vol}(V)=5nk\). For the final inequality, divide \(2/(5k)\) by \(\sqrt{2/(5(n-1))}\) and use \(k\le\pi\sqrt{hn/2}+2\).

Hint for (g). Sort the vertices by \(F\) and look at prefixes, but to match the definition use only threshold sets: skip a prefix boundary if \(F\) is (numerically) tied across it. Here \(F\) takes only \(k\) distinct values.
