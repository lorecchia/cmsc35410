Exercise: Certifying Expansion with the Easy Side of Cheeger

Recommended exercise for Lecture 6

A spectral gap certifies that every cut is large. How tight is this, and how do we check it?

The easy side of Cheeger, \(\lambda\le\bar\phi(G)\le2\Phi(G)\), says that a large gap proves that the graph has no sparse cut. This exercise looks at how good that certificate is, and at what it takes to verify it numerically.

Notation. \(G=(V,E,w)\) connected, \(L=D-A\), \(L_{rw}=D^{-1}L\), \(\lambda=\lambda_2(L_{rw})\), \(\mathrm{vol}=\mathrm{vol}(V)\).

(a) The certificate. Show that for every \(S\subset V\), \[ w(S,\bar S)\ \ge\ \lambda\,\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S)\,\mathrm{vol}(\bar S)}{\mathrm{vol}},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \Phi(S)\ge\frac\lambda2 . \] For a \(\delta\)-regular graph with \(n\) vertices, write this as a bound on the number of edges leaving a set \(S\) with \(|S|\le n/2\).

(b) How tight is it? Compute, by brute force over all \(2^{n-1}-1\) cuts, \(\Phi(G)\) and the ratio \(\Phi(G)/(\lambda/2)\) for:

the Petersen graph, the 4-cube \(Q_4\), \(K_{4,4}\), the cycle \(C_{16}\), and two random \(3\)-regular graphs on \(16\) and \(20\) vertices (use networkx.random_regular_graph with seeds \(1\) and \(2\)).

Which graphs have \(\Phi=\lambda/2\) exactly? What do they have in common? How does the ratio behave for the cycle \(C_n\) as \(n\) grows?

(c) When is the certificate tight? Suppose \(\Phi(S)=\lambda/2\) for some \(S\) with \(\mathrm{vol}(S)=\mathrm{vol}/2\). Show that then \(x=\mathbf 1_S-\mathbf 1_{\bar S}\) is an eigenvector of \(L_{rw}\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda\). Check this on \(Q_d\).

(d) The certificate is a lower bound on \(\lambda\). The power method returns vectors \(x\) with Rayleigh quotient \(R(x)\ge\lambda\) (for \(x\perp_d\mathbf 1\)). Why does this not certify expansion? To certify \(\lambda\ge\ell\) we need a matrix inequality. Show that \(\lambda\ge\ell\) if and only if the symmetric matrix \[ K_\ell=L-\ell\Big(D-\frac{dd^\top}{\mathrm{vol}}\Big) \] is positive semidefinite. Show also that \(K_\ell\mathbf 1=0\), and that \(\lambda>\ell\) if and only if \(K_\ell+\frac1n\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top\) is positive definite. Hence a Cholesky factorization of \(K_\ell+\frac1n\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top\) is a proof that \(\Phi(G)\ge\ell/2\).

(e) Numerics. For a random \(3\)-regular graph on \(n=200\) vertices (seed \(5\)): compute \(\lambda\); run Cholesky on \(K_\ell+\frac1n\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top\) for \(\ell=f\lambda\), \(f\in\{0.9,0.99,0.999,1.001,1.01,1.1\}\); compute the Rayleigh quotient of a random mean-zero vector. Summarize: which side does each of the two computations give information about?

(f) The interval. Combining everything, for the graph of (e): by (d) there is a proven lower bound \(\Phi(G)\ge\ell/2\), and by the sweep (Lecture 6, upper side) a cut with \(\Phi(S)\le\sqrt{2\lambda}\). Report the interval \([\ell/2,\ \Phi(S)]\) obtained with \(\ell=0.999\lambda\) and the actual sweep cut of the Fiedler vector, and its width as a ratio.

Hint for (a). Apply the Poincaré inequality \(\lambda\,\mathrm{Var}_\pi(x)\le\mathcal E(x,x)\) to \(x=\mathbf 1_S\), as in the proof of the easy side. For \(\delta\)-regular graphs, \(\mathrm{vol}(S)=\delta|S|\) and \(\mathrm{vol}(\bar S)\ge\mathrm{vol}/2\).

Hint for (b). For \(n\le20\), enumerate the subsets with a vectorized loop over bit masks (chunks of \(2^{16}\) at a time); only the first \(n-1\) vertices need to vary.

Hint for (c). Equality in \(\lambda\le\bar\phi(S)\) requires equality in the Poincaré inequality for \(x=\mathbf 1_S\), i.e. \(x-\bar x\mathbf 1\) must be a minimizer of the Rayleigh quotient, hence an eigenvector for \(\lambda\).

Hint for (d). \(x^\top K_\ell x=\mathcal E(x,x)-\ell\,\|x-\bar x\mathbf 1\|_d^2\), where \(\bar x=\langle x,\mathbf 1\rangle_d/\mathrm{vol}\). Also \(K_\ell\mathbf 1=L\mathbf 1-\ell(d-d)=0\).

Hint for (f). Use the threshold-set sweep of the Fiedler vector of \(L_{rw}\), with \(f=D^{-1/2}v_2\).
