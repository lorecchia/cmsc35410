Exercise: Three Clusters and the Spectral Embedding

Preview exercise for Lecture 7 (higher-order Cheeger)

When there are k clusters, one eigenvector is not enough: λ₂,…,λ_k are all small.

Cheeger’s inequality is about one bottleneck. Real graphs often have \(k\) clusters. Here you will study the simplest example, a ring of three cliques, and see how the spectrum signals \(k=3\), why a single sweep cut is not the right tool, and how the spectral embedding into \(\mathbb R^2\) reveals the clusters.

The graph. \(G_m\) consists of three disjoint cliques \(C_0,C_1,C_2\) on \(m\) vertices each. Join them in a ring by three bridge edges: vertex \(0\) of \(C_i\) to vertex \(1\) of \(C_{i+1}\) (indices mod \(3\)). All weights are \(1\). So \(n=3m\), and each clique has \(\mathrm{vol}(C_i)=m(m-1)+2\).

For \(k\ge2\), the \(k\)-way conductance is \[ \rho_k(G)=\min_{S_1,\dots,S_k\ \text{disjoint, nonempty}}\ \max_i\Phi(S_i). \] Higher-order Cheeger (Lee–Oveis Gharan–Trevisan; Louis–Raghavendra–Tetali–Vempala) states that \(\ \dfrac{\lambda_k}{2}\le\rho_k(G)\le O(k^2)\sqrt{\lambda_k}\), where \(\lambda_k=\lambda_k(L_{rw})\).

(a) The 3-way conductance. Show that \(\rho_3(G_m)\le\dfrac{2}{m(m-1)+2}\). Compute this for \(m=8,12\).

(b) The spectrum. Using a test function that is constant on each clique, show that \(\lambda_2,\lambda_3\le\dfrac{3}{m(m-1)+2}\). (Take \(y=(y_0,y_1,y_2)\) with \(\sum_iy_i=0\); use \(\sum_i y_iy_{i+1}=-\frac12\sum_iy_i^2\).) Explain, using the rotation symmetry \(C_i\to C_{i+1}\), why one expects \(\lambda_2=\lambda_3\). Compute the first five eigenvalues of \(L_{rw}\) numerically for \(m=8,12\).

(c) One eigenvector, one cut. Take a random vector \(f\) in the span of the second and third eigenfunctions of \(L_{rw}\) (use \(f=D^{-1/2}v\), with \(v\) an eigenvector of the normalized adjacency). Compute its best sweep cut. What cut is it? Is it also a good 3-way partition? What is \(\Phi(G_m)\)?

(d) The spectral embedding. Map each vertex \(i\) to \(F(i)=(f_2(i),f_3(i))\in\mathbb R^2\), where \(f_2,f_3\) are the eigenfunctions of (b). Plot the \(3m\) points. Report the distance between the centers of the three clique clusters and the maximum distance of a point to its cluster center, for \(m=8,12\). How would you round this embedding to a 3-way partition, and what is the conductance of the result?

(e) The eigengap. Compute \(\lambda_4\). Use the lower bound \(\rho_4\ge\lambda_4/2\) to show that no 4-way partition of \(G_m\) has all parts of conductance below \(\approx0.5\). Compare with \(\rho_3\). This is the “eigengap heuristic” for choosing \(k\): we pick \(k\) such that \(\lambda_k\) is small and \(\lambda_{k+1}\) is not.

(f) Discussion. (1) For \(k=2\) the sweep rounding gives a set; for \(k=3\) the embedding is \(2\)-dimensional and one must cluster a point cloud. Where does the “loss” in higher-order Cheeger, the factor poly\((k)\), come from? (2) What would happen if you tried to find three clusters by repeating the two-way sweep on \(G_m\) (cut off the best set, recurse on the rest)? Compare with the planted partition in (a).

Hint for (a). Take \(S_i=C_i\). Two bridge edges leave each clique.

Hint for (b). For \(y\) extended to a vector constant on cliques, the only non-zero terms of \(\sum_Ew(x_i-x_j)^2\) are the three bridges, giving \(\sum_i(y_i-y_{i+1})^2=3\sum_iy_i^2\), while \(\|x\|_d^2=\mathrm{vol}(C_0)\sum_iy_i^2\). For the symmetry: the rotation \(\sigma\) commutes with \(L_{rw}\), so \(L_{rw}\) preserves the eigenspaces of \(\sigma\) (eigenvalues \(1,\omega,\bar\omega\)). The \(\omega\) and \(\bar\omega\) eigenspaces are complex conjugate, and have the same spectrum.

Hint for (c). Use np.linalg.eigh(np.eye(n) - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}). For the sweep, use threshold sets.

Hint for (d). To round: pick the three clusters by angle, or run \(k\)-means with \(k=3\) on \(F(V)\).

Hint for (e). In the notation of the statement, \(\lambda_4\) is the fourth smallest eigenvalue of \(L_{rw}\) counting \(\lambda_1=0\).
