Exercise: Why the Median? A Counterexample to the Naive Rounding

Recommended exercise for Lecture 6

Lemma 3 shifts the eigenvector by the median. Here is what goes wrong without it.

In the proof of Cheeger’s inequality, Lemma 3 replaces the Fiedler vector \(f\perp_d\mathbf 1\) by a nonnegative vector \(f'\) that is supported on a set of \(\pi\)-measure at most \(\frac12\). This looks like a technicality. You will show that it is not: the “obvious” shortcut of taking the positive part of \(f\) fails, by an arbitrarily large factor.

Notation. As in the lecture, \(R_2(x)=\dfrac{\sum_Ew_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2}{\sum_id_ix_i^2}\) and \(R_1(x)=\dfrac{\sum_Ew_{ij}|x_i-x_j|}{\sum_id_i|x_i|}\) (both uncentered), \(S_t=\{x>t\}\), and for a set \(S\), \(\Phi(S)=\dfrac{w(S,\bar S)}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}(S),\mathrm{vol}(\bar S)\}}\).

The graph. \(G_{a,b}\) consists of a clique \(K_a\) and a clique \(K_b\), \(a\gg b\), joined by a single edge. Take \(b=10\) and \(a=900\). Let \(A\) be the vertex set of \(K_a\) and \(B\) that of \(K_b\).

(a) Basic quantities. Compute \(\mathrm{vol}(A)\), \(\mathrm{vol}(B)\), and \(\Phi(B)\). Compute \(\lambda\) numerically. Conclude with the easy side of Cheeger that every cut of \(G\) has \(\Phi\ge\lambda/2\approx0.005\).

(b) A vector with mean zero. Let \(x=\mathbf 1_A-c\,\mathbf 1_B\) with \(c=\mathrm{vol}(A)/\mathrm{vol}(B)\), so that \(x\perp_d\mathbf 1\). Compute \(R_2(x)\) (exactly, in terms of \(\mathrm{vol}(A)\) and \(\mathrm{vol}(B)\)) and compare with \(\lambda\). This \(x\) is a good stand-in for the Fiedler vector.

(c) The naive rounding fails. Let \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}=\mathbf 1_A\). Compute \(R_2(x_+)\) and \(R_1(x_+^2)\), and the set \(S\) returned by the sweep of \(x_+^2\). Show that

  1. \(R_2(x_+)\ll\lambda\), so the conclusion “\(\Phi(S_t)\le\sqrt{2R_2(x_+)}\) for some \(t\)” would be false: give the numerical values of \(\sqrt{2R_2(x_+)}\) and \(\lambda/2\).
  2. Which step of the chain Lemma 2, then Lemma 1, still holds for \(x_+\), and which hypothesis of the final identification \(w(S_t,\bar S_t)/\mathrm{vol}(S_t)=\Phi(S_t)\) breaks?

(d) The median fixes it. Compute the \(\pi\)-median \(m\) of \(x\), the vector \(\bar x=x-m\mathbf 1\), its positive and negative parts \(p,q\), and \(R_2(p)\), \(R_2(q)\). Verify \(R_2(\bar x)\ge\min\{R_2(p),R_2(q)\}\) and \(\min\{R_2(p),R_2(q)\}\le R_2(x)\). Which set does the sweep of \(q^2\) return, and what is its conductance?

(e) Mean versus median. The mean of \(x\) is already \(0\) in \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_d\), so \(x\) itself is “centered”. Explain why centering at the mean does not give the support condition \(\pi(\{f'>0\})\le\frac12\), but centering at the median does. Construct a vector \(y\) on the path \(P_3\) (three vertices) with \(y\perp_d\mathbf 1\) for which \(\pi(\{y>0\})>\frac12\).

(f) What exactly is the median used for? In Lemma 3, writing \(\bar f=p-q\) with \(p,q\ge0\), the inequality \(N(p-q)\ge N(p)+N(q)\) and the identity \(D(p-q)=D(p)+D(q)\) hold for any shift, as long as \(p,q\) have disjoint supports. State precisely which conclusion of Lemma 3 needs the median, and which needs the identity \(\sum_id_if_i=0\).

Hint for (a). \(\mathrm{vol}(A)=a(a-1)+1\), \(\mathrm{vol}(B)=b(b-1)+1\). Compute \(\lambda\) with np.linalg.eigvalsh on \(I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}\).

Hint for (b). \(N(x)=\sum_Ew(x_i-x_j)^2\) is just the bridge: \((1+c)^2\). And \(\|x\|_d^2=\mathrm{vol}(A)+c^2\mathrm{vol}(B)\).

Hint for (c). \(x_+\) is constant on \(A\) and \(0\) on \(B\): only the bridge contributes to the numerator. \(S=A\) has \(w(S,\bar S)/\mathrm{vol}(S)=1/\mathrm{vol}(A)\), but \(\Phi(S)\) uses \(\min\{\mathrm{vol}(A),\mathrm{vol}(B)\}=\mathrm{vol}(B)\).

Hint for (d). Since \(\pi(A)>\frac12\) and \(x=1\) on \(A\), the median is \(m=1\). Then \(\bar x=0\) on \(A\) and \(-(1+c)\) on \(B\).

Hint for (e). On \(P_3=\{1,2,3\}\) the degrees are \(d=(1,2,1)\). Try \(y=(-a,1,b)\) with \(b>0\) and solve \(\langle y,\mathbf 1\rangle_d=0\) for \(a\).
