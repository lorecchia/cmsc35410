Exercise: Leighton–Rao as a Linear Program

Recommended exercise for Lecture 6

Relax sparsest cut to all metrics: an LP, its flow dual, and when it is exact.

In Lecture 6, \(\lambda\) was the relaxation of sparsest cut obtained by replacing \(\ell_1\) by \(\ell_2^2\). Here you will build the other classical relaxation: keep the triangle inequality, drop \(\ell_1\). It is a linear program, its dual is a multicommodity flow problem, and you can solve it on small graphs and see exactly when it is tight.

Setup. \(G=(V,E,w)\) is a connected weighted graph, \(n=|V|\), with \(d_i=\sum_jw_{ij}\) and \(\mathrm{vol}(V)=\sum_id_i\). Put \(c_{ij}=d_id_j/\mathrm{vol}(V)\). For a symmetric \(d:V\times V\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) with \(d_{ii}=0\), write \[ d(G)=\sum_{ij\in E}w_{ij}\,d_{ij},\qquad d(K_G)=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\,d_{ij}. \] The Leighton–Rao relaxation is \[ \mathrm{LR}(G)=\min\Big\{\,d(G)\ :\ d(K_G)=1,\ \ d_{ij}\le d_{ik}+d_{kj}\ \ \forall i,j,k,\ \ d\ge0\Big\}. \] Recall \(\bar\phi(S)=\dfrac{w(S,\bar S)\,\mathrm{vol}(V)}{\mathrm{vol}(S)\mathrm{vol}(\bar S)}\) and \(\bar\phi(G)=\min_S\bar\phi(S)\), with \(\Phi\le\bar\phi\le2\Phi\).

(a) It is a relaxation. For a cut \(S\), let \(\delta^S_{ij}=\mathbf 1[i,j\text{ separated by }S]\). Show that \(\bar\phi(S)=\delta^S(G)/\delta^S(K_G)\), that \(\delta^S\) is a metric, and conclude \[ \mathrm{LR}(G)\le\bar\phi(G). \] Also show that the constraint \(d(K_G)=1\) can be replaced by minimizing the ratio: \(\mathrm{LR}(G)=\min_{d\text{ metric}}d(G)/d(K_G)\).

(b) A graph where it is strictly smaller. Let \(G=K_{2,3}\) with unit weights.

  1. Compute \(\bar\phi(G)\) by finding the best cut (there are only a few types of cuts, up to symmetry).
  2. Let \(d\) be the shortest-path metric of \(G\) (\(d_{ij}=1\) on edges, \(2\) for non-adjacent pairs). Compute \(d(G)/d(K_G)\) and conclude \(\mathrm{LR}(G)<\bar\phi(G)\).
  3. Using the remark after Lemma 1 (every \(\ell_1\) metric \(d\) has a cut with \(\bar\phi(S)\le d(G)/d(K_G)\)), deduce that the shortest-path metric of \(K_{2,3}\) is not \(\ell_1\)-embeddable.

(c) Solve it. Implement the LP for unit-weight graphs with scipy.optimize.linprog (variables \(d_{ij}\), \(i<j\); one equality; \(3\binom n3\) triangle inequalities). For each graph below, compute \(\lambda=\lambda_2(L_{rw})\), \(\Phi\), \(\bar\phi\) (by brute force over cuts), and \(\mathrm{LR}\):

\(K_4\), \(C_8\), \(C_{10}\), \(Q_3\) (the 3-cube), the complete binary tree of depth 3, the Petersen graph, the barbell (two \(K_4\) joined by one edge), \(K_{2,3}\), \(K_{3,3}\).

For which graphs is \(\mathrm{LR}=\bar\phi\)? Do you see a pattern? Is \(\mathrm{LR}\) always larger or always smaller than \(\lambda\)?

(d) Trees. Show that \(\mathrm{LR}(G)=\bar\phi(G)\) whenever \(G\) is a tree. Do it in two steps:

  1. The minimum in the definition of \(\mathrm{LR}\) is attained by the shortest-path metric of some edge lengths \(\ell\ge0\).
  2. The shortest-path metric of a tree is a nonnegative combination of cut metrics.

(e) The dual is a flow problem. Consider the maximum concurrent flow problem: for every pair \(u<v\), we want to send \(\alpha\,c_{uv}\) units of flow from \(u\) to \(v\), along any collection of paths, such that the total flow through each edge \(e\in E\) is at most its capacity \(w_e\). Let \(\alpha^*\) be the largest feasible \(\alpha\).

  1. (Weak duality, directly.) Show \(\alpha^*\le\bar\phi(G)\) by looking at the flow crossing a cut.
  2. (Strong duality.) Write the flow problem as an LP in the path variables \(f_P\ge0\), take its dual, and show that it equals \(\mathrm{LR}(G)\). Conclude \(\alpha^*=\mathrm{LR}(G)\).
  3. (Lecture 5.) Explain how a canonical path routing with congestion \(\kappa\) yields a feasible flow, hence a certificate \(\bar\phi(G)\ge 1/\kappa'\) for a suitable \(\kappa'\) you should define. How is this different from the certificate \(\lambda_2(L)\ge2n/\kappa\) of Lecture 5?

(f) Rounding (reading). Bourgain’s theorem says that every \(n\)-point metric embeds into \(\ell_1\) with distortion \(O(\log n)\). Using Lemma 1, explain how this turns an optimal solution \(d^*\) of the LP into a cut \(S\) with \(\bar\phi(S)\le O(\log n)\cdot\mathrm{LR}(G)\le O(\log n)\cdot\bar\phi(G)\). What changes if only the objective (not each pair) needs to be preserved, as in the proof of Cheeger?

Hint for (a). Check the triangle inequality for \(\delta^S\) by cases on which side each of \(i,j,k\) lies on. For scale-invariance, note that the constraint set is a cone and the objective is homogeneous.

Hint for (b). For \(K_{2,3}\), the vertices are \(a_1,a_2\) (degree \(3\)) and \(b_1,b_2,b_3\) (degree \(2\)), \(\mathrm{vol}(V)=12\). Try \(S=\{a_1\},\{b_1\},\{a_1,a_2\},\{a_1,b_1\}\). For the shortest-path metric, split the pairs into \(a\)–\(a\), \(b\)–\(b\), and \(a\)–\(b\) types.

Hint for (c). For the triangle inequalities, loop over ordered triples \((i,j,k)\) and add the row \(d_{jk}-d_{ij}-d_{ik}\le0\). A helper P(i,j) returning the index of the pair \(\{i,j\}\) makes this short.

Hint for (d). For step 1: given an optimal metric \(d\), set \(\ell_{ij}=d_{ij}\) on edges and let \(\hat d\) be the shortest-path metric of \(\ell\). Compare numerators and denominators (use the triangle inequality for \(d\) along paths). For step 2: removing an edge \(e\) of the tree splits \(V\) into \(S_e\) and \(\bar S_e\), and the path from \(u\) to \(v\) uses \(e\) iff \(S_e\) separates them.

Hint for (e). For 2, the primal has one capacity constraint per edge (dual variable \(\ell_e\ge0\)) and one demand constraint per pair (dual variable \(z_{uv}\ge0\)). Show that, at the optimum, \(z_{uv}\) is the shortest-path distance under the lengths \(\ell\), then reuse step 1 of (d).
